01 About

I work at the intersection of LLM evaluation and reinforcement learning. My background is in preference-optimization methods (DPO / APO / RLOO) from my time as a visiting researcher with KAUST's OPTIMAL Lab, and most of my recent work has been building benchmarks that expose a specific failure mode: an answer, a caption, or a judge's score that reads as correct but isn't — in Arabic visual question answering, in culturally grounded reasoning, and in LLM-as-judge evaluation more broadly.

That question — when does a learning signal look trustworthy while being wrong? — is what's pulling me toward RL. I've started building and running independent multi-agent RL experiments to understand how the same problem shows up when the "signal" is a reward, not a label, and whether the fixes that work for one transfer to the other.

Most of this started from a stubborn habit of not trusting a result until I've audited it by hand — on every benchmark I've built, the thing that ended up mattering was the case I almost didn't bother checking.

03 Research focus

Trustworthy learning signals

Human feedback, LLM-judge scores, and multi-agent reward sharing all share a failure mode: the signal can look reliable and still be wrong. I design evaluation protocols that catch this by hand-auditing, not just trusting the aggregate score.

Culturally grounded evaluation

Benchmarks built specifically for Arabic language and Gulf / Islamic cultural context, where off-the-shelf English benchmarks and translation-based evaluation quietly miss the failure modes that matter.

Multi-agent reinforcement learning

Independent Q-learning in sequential social dilemmas — when cooperation emerges from self-interested learning alone, when it doesn't, and what reward shaping actually does to agents that can't see each other.

04 Projects

Fatan

LLM Evaluation

Culturally Aligned vs. Culturally Competent — a benchmark testing whether multimodal LLMs genuinely understand Gulf/Islamic cultural context or just pattern-match plausible answers. Three-tier design (MCQ, open-ended, "none of the above") built specifically to catch models that guess well on multiple choice but fall apart once the safety net of a correct option is removed.

Dataset ↗

Sarab

LLM Evaluation

An Arabic cause-diagnostic visual-hallucination benchmark — testing not just whether a model hallucinates on Arabic visual questions but why: visual ambiguity, multimodal inconsistency, or incorrect context, each isolated by construction rather than inferred after the fact. 400+ culturally grounded images, fully original cultural counter-common-sense set.

Repository ↗

social-dilemma-rl

Reinforcement Learning

Independent tabular Q-learning agents in sequential social dilemmas — iterated matrix games and a spatial tragedy-of-the-commons gridworld. Includes a real, reported negative result: inequity-averse reward shaping made resource collapse worse here, with a concrete hypothesis for why, instead of a result quietly dropped.
